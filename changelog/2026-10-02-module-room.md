# Modules take up room

- **Every module has a box:** as much room as its volume, proportioned by what it is (a drive
  long, a lift flat, racks broad, a gun long and thin): `Module::dims`.
- **Placed in the hull** (`place`, at assembly): each module goes to the free spot nearest its
  slot's mount where its box is wholly inside the hull and clear of every other module.
  - The biggest go first.
  - The search tries along the ship first, then up or down, sideways last, so a layout stays
    symmetric and the balance on the centreline.
- **Balance follows where they sit:** the centre of mass and inertia come from the modules'
  places, not their mounts.
- **No room anywhere: it won't go together.** A hull, a refit or a design whose module can't fit
  is refused: "NO ROOM FOR FUEL TANK 80 T (120 M3)". The planner and designer say so.
- **The drawings show them:** the planner's and designer's views draw every module as a box
  where it sits. The one being chosen (PLAN page), or those the knob moves (DESIGN page: TANK AT
  lights the tank, ENGINE ROOM AT the plant, drive and hyperdrive), are bright.
- **The stock hulls made to fit:**
  - Everything fits; modules sit within 1.6–3.3 m of their mounts.
  - The interceptor's fuselage is a quarter fatter (life support and thrusters had no room in
    the needle).
  - The bulk hauler's hold now sits over its centre of mass, so it flies the same loaded or
    empty.
  - The stock thruster layouts are rebalanced about the new centres.
- Tests:
  - every stock hull's modules wholly inside it, none overlapping;
  - a 16 m sliver asked to carry size-4 tanks and racks won't go together (NO ROOM).
