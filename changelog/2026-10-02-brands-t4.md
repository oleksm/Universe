# Brands, where modules are sold, what they're built from (T4)

- **Brands** (`brands.ron`): Kestrel Driveworks (drives and thrusters: light, strong, dear),
  Hadley Heavy Industries (plants, tanks, racks: rugged, cheap), Orbital Systems (computers,
  sensors, navigation), Cormorant Mining. Modules name their maker; the basics are unbranded and
  made anywhere. A Kestrel K2 drive joins the range (1.5 MN a nozzle, light, 68,000 CR).
- **The outfitter** (`services::outfitter`) works everything out from the seed and the charts,
  so the world and the shipyard panel agree:
  - **a brand's home** is a settled system with a station, picked from the seed by its key;
    there, its whole range is carried;
  - **farther off**, a module is carried less often (×0.8 a gate hop, down to 15%) and costs more
    (+5% a hop, for shipping);
  - **a module is built from goods**: electronics for the computing kind, machinery for the rest,
    by its mass. Fitting one takes that from the station's stock (none in stock, none to fit),
    and one taken out puts half back. Shipbuilding is now a sink for the economy's production
    and hauling.
- **Refitting** follows it: "NOT CARRIED HERE", "OUT OF MACHINERY TO BUILD IT", the local price.
- **The shipyard panel** shows, for each module, its price here (or "-" if it isn't carried,
  "NO STOCK" if there's nothing to build it from). For the one picked: its maker and what the
  maker is known for, what it does, and "MADE HERE" or "2 GATES FROM ITS MAKER'S HOME (+10%)".
  The layout fits the screen (seen in frames: the cargo and drive slots).
- **Tests:**
  - a brand sells its whole range at home at list price; away, fewer stations carry it, each at
    +5% a hop;
  - a refit is charged the local price and takes its machinery from the station (0.8 t built,
    half of the old 1.5 t back).
