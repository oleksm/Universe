# Cheap where it's made, dear where it's needed

- **Settled markets priced everything from the same catalogue price,** moved only by stock.
  With both places at their usual stock, a farm world and a station priced food the same: no
  money in hauling it.
- **Now a place prices goods by its relation to them:**
  - where it makes them, 0.75× the catalogue price;
  - where it needs them, 1.3×;
  - both still moved by stock (short dear, up to 3×; a glut cheap).
  The difference pays for the haul: at the usual stock, food bought at a farm world for 18 sells
  at a station for 29 (+65%), more where it's short.
- **A maker keeps three days of its own use before it sells any,** so a hungry place doesn't
  sell you its last food.
- A maker sells 5% over its price and buys back 15% under it: dumping goods back where you
  bought them loses money.
- Test: food bought where it's made sells dearer where it's needed, and back where it was bought
  for less.
