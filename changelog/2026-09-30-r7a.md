## The cockpit (`sim::cockpit`)

- **The player's pilot and ship computers are one client,** the `Cockpit`:
  - the avionics (a `Pilot`, thinking with the human at the stick: it flies by the stick
    unless a program has it, and hunts no one);
  - the radar picture (the view's ships, with the crafts' `Transponder`s), and the lock;
  - fire control (it lays the gun by posting);
  - the flight plan (only when a display wants it: `plan_wanted`);
  - the collision warning, approach guidance and follow status.
- **It flies the ship only by posting,** exactly as an NPC pilot does: commands due two ticks
  after the view it read. The human's requests (throttle, thrusters, hyperdrive, nav target,
  clearance, autopilot, route, follow, lock) run at once against the latest view, and post
  likewise.
- **The world keeps the player's ship as a body:** its inbox, its pilot's published status
  (for traffic control and the recorder), and the feed of what happened to it. `Universe` holds
  the cockpit and thinks it in step with the world (tests, and the engine for now). The client
  takes it in R7b.
- **The pilot view lists every ship by combat id** (the player's 0, craft i: i + 1), so one view
  serves the NPC pool and the cockpit.
- **Removed:** the player's in-step turn (`Vessel`, `Link`), and the engine's own ship
  computers. `Universe::step` (the player's ship alone) is internal; use `step_world`.

## A new core rule: no lift-off on orders given before touching down

Under the delay, the landing autopilot's braking thrust from the ticks before touchdown arrived
after it and lifted the ship back off. Ships record when they lock down (`Ship::locked_at`), and
postings carry when their pilot saw the world (`Posting::seen`). An order given before the lock
doesn't touch a landed ship's engine or thrusters (`vessel::Inbox::deliver`).

## Tests

All the player's autopilot flights pass at the 2-tick delay (R7's exit check):
- docking from spawn;
- landing from orbit;
- the gate run;
- hyperdrive to a port;
- the plan to pad and slot;
- the route.

Three tests were adjusted where they relied on instant effect:
- the throttle is ordered after the drive is engaged;
- a few ticks pass before planning, for the pad to be assigned;

# R7b — the cockpit on the client side

- **Once the engine runs on its own thread, `EngineHandle` takes the cockpit.** A world-link
  thread on the client side feeds it every tick's `CockpitView`, along with what happened to
  the ship (`Universe::cockpit_out`), and sends its postings back (`Command::Post`, which
  `Universe::accept` applies at their due ticks).
- **The player's commands go to the cockpit on the client side** (`EngineHandle::send`):
  throttle, thrusters, stick, hyperdrive, nav target, clearance, autopilot, route, follow,
  lock, collision warning. Walking, trades, warp and respawn still go to the engine.
- **The HUD reads the cockpit's displays,** merged into the view (`View::with_cockpit`). Saves
  take the avionics from the cockpit (`EngineHandle::save` / `load`, `Universe::save_with`).
- **`UNIVERSE_COCKPIT_IN_ENGINE=1`** keeps it in the engine, as the tests do.
- **Test:** `the_cockpit_flies_the_ship_from_the_client_side`. Through a real engine thread, a
  throttle and a nav target given to the client's cockpit reach the ship.

# R7c — prediction

- **The cockpit predicts what its orders on their way will do** (`Cockpit::predict`). It steps
  the ship through the ticks until they're all due with the shared kernel (its own `World`
  from the same seed), with the orders and without, and takes the difference in position and
  turn. The client adds that to the ship it draws, and to the camera (`App::place`), so the
  stick shows at once though its orders take effect two ticks later.
- **Only the difference is predicted, never the absolute position:** the world is drawn
  interpolated in heliocentric coordinates, and an absolutely predicted ship would sit
  kilometres off its planet.
- **Test:** `the_cockpit_predicts_what_its_orders_will_do_to_the_tick`. With the stick pushed,
  the ship hasn't turned yet but the prediction has. When the order lands two ticks later, the
  ship is where the prediction said, to within 1e-9 rad.

R7 is done: the player is a client under the same contract as the NPCs.
