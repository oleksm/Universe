## Sunlight

- **Engine `Light`**: a star's position, colour (brightest channel 1) and luminosity (brightness 1
  at `reference` metres). Brightness falls with the square of distance, and the eye adapts part of
  the way: perceived brightness = irradiance^`EXPOSURE` (0.3), clamped 0.1–1.6. A planet at
  0.4 AU from a sun-like star is bright, one at 5 AU about 0.4, one at 30 AU about 0.1.
- **In the scene**: the system's star is the light, with its class's colour and luminosity
  (mass^3.5 in solar units, 1 at 1 AU for a G star). So an M dwarf's worlds are dim and red-lit,
  and an O/B giant's brilliant and blue-white.
- **Shading**: faces are the base colour × (ambient + Lambert × the light's colour and brightness
  there); edges likewise. Night is darker: face ambient 0.06 (was 0.12), edge ambient 0.25 (was
  0.3), so night sides are dark but shapes stay readable. The ground grid up close uses the same
  brightness.
- **The sky, day and night**: inside the atmosphere (below 100 km) of a world with air (Terran),
  the sky takes the sun's light by its elevation:
  - blue by day, with the sun's tint and brightness;
  - red and orange at twilight (around the horizon);
  - black at night;
  - thinning out with altitude.

  Stars wash out by day (97%) and come back at night. Airless worlds and space have a black sky
  with the stars always showing, as it really is.
- **The star itself** is drawn in its full colour; it was 30%, which looked dark against a lit sky.
- Dev scenarios `noon`, `dusk`, `night`: 2 km over the home planet, looking along the ground, with
  the sun overhead, on the horizon, and below it.
