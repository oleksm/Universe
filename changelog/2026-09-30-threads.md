# 2026-09-30 — Client/engine split, phases 2–5: commands and views, the world on its own threads

(Continues `2026-09-30-gpu-meshes.md`, phase 1.)

## Phase 2 — the boundary

- **2a. `Rc` → `Arc`** throughout the world engine: the universe can move to another thread.
- **2b. `universe_sim::engine`**:
  - `Command`: everything the client does to the world. Ship settings, throttle changes and
    thrusters, the stick, warp, walking, hyperdrive, nav target, clearance, autopilot, route
    (on, push, pop, clear, random), follow, lock, collision warning, respawn, market watch and
    trade.
  - `View`: what the client reads after a tick. The player's ship, avionics and crew; other
    ships as drawn; weapons fire; kills and trades; events since the last view; the ship
    computers' outputs; the turrets and pads of the player's system.
  - `Engine` runs the player's ship computers in the world engine: radar, fire control, the
    flight plan (its cadence and cost), and the collision warning.
  - Locks and trades answer with events (`Lock`, `NothingInBeam`, `ContactLost`, `Traded`).
- **2c. `world::charts::Charts`**: the galaxy, gate network, goods and star systems, generated
  from the seed. Shared read-only by the engine and the client; the client generates the
  systems it shows itself.
- **The client**:
  - holds an `EngineHandle`, the latest `View` and the `Charts`;
  - turns input into commands;
  - has no other access to the universe, except dev scenarios setting it up before the engine
    starts.

## Phase 3 — the world engine on its own thread

- `EngineHandle::start`: the engine ticks at 60 Hz on its own thread.
  - Commands are applied before each tick, and a view is posted after it.
  - A view the client didn't take passes its events and hits on to the next one.
- **Client**:
  - takes the newest view each frame, and handles its events only once;
  - carries motion forward between ticks (positions by velocity, attitude by spin, body
    positions by time), so a high refresh rate doesn't judder;
  - sends the throttle as changes, not values computed from a stale view;
  - saves and loads through a blocking call on the engine.
- `UNIVERSE_ENGINE_THREAD=0` runs it in the client's thread, for debugging.

## Phase 4 — crafts side by side

- **The world steps a ship through shared access**:
  - caches (systems, neighbours, body positions, the integrator's snapshot, turrets) moved
    behind locks;
  - the ship step takes an explicit clock (`step_ship_at`);
  - no shared scratch buffer.
- **`World::freeze`**: before the crafts step, what they'll look up is gathered once and read
  without locks. With locks alone, 32 threads queued on them: the crafts pass took 8.5 ms per
  tick at 10,000 ships; frozen, about 1.2 ms.
- **The crafts step on all cores (`rayon`)** through `FrameLink`: a bus over the shared world,
  with each ship's own clock.
  - Traffic control's answers come from the last tick (`peek_pad`, `peek_corridor`). The
    requests are made after, in craft order, so the outcome doesn't depend on thread timing
    (one tick of latency).
  - Events, hunts begun and ended, fleeing, crashes and trades are applied in craft order
    after.
- **Traffic presence** is worked out in parallel.
- **Collisions** compare only pairs where something moves. Hundreds of landed ships at a port
  were being compared pairwise every tick.
- **The profiler** keeps a tally per thread (uncontended), gathered at the end of each frame.

## Phase 5 — the render thread

- The engine crate's render thread owns the GPU.
  - It takes finished frames over a one-slot channel, then uploads, submits and presents them.
  - It handles resizes and screenshots, and reports sizes and timings back.
- The main thread does window, input, update and frame building, and goes on to the next frame
  while the last is presented.

## Measured (headless crowd scenario, 32 cores)

- **1,000 ships**:
  - client main thread: about 0.5 ms update and 0.5 ms draw per frame;
  - world tick: about 1.6 ms, of which crafts 0.6 ms (it was 1.7 ms serial).
- **10,000 ships**: world tick about 5 ms, comfortably inside 60 Hz.
  - Before: 23 ms, not reaching 60 Hz.
  - Now: crafts about 1.3 ms, traffic presence about 1.4 ms, building the view about 0.8 ms,
    collisions about 0.5 ms.
