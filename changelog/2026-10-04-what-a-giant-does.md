# What a giant does to its moons

- **Tidal heat:** a moon of a giant is kneaded on its stretched orbit, and the heat comes out
  through its surface: 21/2 (k2/Q) G M² R⁵ n e² / a⁶, with the registry's k2/Q (`seeding.conditions`).
  It warms the moon in the climate model (mean, by place, and through the day), and the registry's
  thresholds tell slight, a buried sea, volcanic, or past any moon known.
- **Radiation:** the dose round a gas giant (Sv/s), as Jupiter's at the record's distance, falling
  with distance as its power, in proportion to the giant's mass; and whether it's deadly
  unshielded. Round Treistun j its inner moons take 30 to 130 Sv a day.
- **Treistun's four moons on orbits that couldn't last** now show it. Treistun i I (1,866 W/m²)
  averages 153 °C, j I (531 W/m²) 38 °C, h I and i II about −100 °C, where sunlight alone leaves
  them near −200 °C. These are the seed's orbits (first moons too close, too stretched); the figures
  match the registry's.
- The engine's figures for the registry's page (`registry-figures`) now carry each moon's tidal
  heat, its state, the dose and its mean temperature.
