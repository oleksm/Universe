# One-way gates, facing each other, in clear space

- **Each gate faces the star it leads to:** its axis, the way through, points at that star in
  the galaxy, and its twin there faces back.
- **One way:**
  - A gate takes you only when you cross it along its axis, toward where it leads.
  - Crossing it the other way, it's an empty ring: you fly through and stay ("the wrong way:
    through an empty ring").
  - You come out of the twin still moving the same way through space, on its entry side. Turn
    round and go back through, and you're home.
- **Clear paths:**
  - A gate's orbit round its planet is picked in a gap: 100 km clear of every moon's,
    station's and asteroid field's band of orbit (fields by their extent), and of the other
    gates'.
  - Its run-in and its way out never pass near anything large. Tested at 16 moments through
    each gate's orbit in the gate network: nothing within 50 km of 8 km either side of it.
- **The transit autopilot runs in from the entry side.** From the far side, it gets onto the
  axis and goes back through the empty ring rather than round the tube. The flight planner flies
  on through a crossing that takes it nowhere, as the world does.
- **The guide marks the way through:** the run-in point behind the ring and arrowheads along the
  axis. A ship leaving through a gate streaks out along its axis.
- Tests:
  - the gate faces its destination and its twin faces back;
  - crossing the wrong way, nothing happens;
  - through it, the ship keeps its motion through space (turned half round relative to the twin);
  - the clear-path check above.
