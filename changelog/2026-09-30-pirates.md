# 2026-09-30 — Pirates, and a kill feed

User: "pirate settlers, who are hunting other settlers. Normal settlers keep the travel program.
Pirates find a settler, usually around landing zones, then chase and destroy, sticking to one as
long as they can. About 10% of settlers become pirates." Then: "when a ship is blown, print an
event line of who destroyed whom with what (last shot)."

## The hunter (avionics `hunter`)

A pirate is a settler with the hunter program installed (`Avionics::pirate`). About 1 in 10,
chosen from each settler's seed, so the same ones every run.

- It flies its route like a trader, docking and landing at stations and spaceports, which puts it
  where the traffic is. While flying in normal space, not resting after a hunt (`REST` = 60 s),
  it watches its radar picture (`Sighting`s from the sim: other crafts in radar range, whether
  they're pirates, sheltered, wrecked or in hyperdrive). A trader in the open within
  `HUNT_RANGE` = 20 km becomes its prey.
- The hunt:
  1. Drop the route and any clearance, then arm.
  2. Chase: close at up to 400 m/s, braking in time to stop at a 900 m standoff, with the
     engine pointed where the push is needed and thrusters for the rest.
  3. Attack, within about 3.9 km: nose on the fire-control lead, thrusters holding the range, the
     gun when the lead is inside the gimbal (under 3 km), the laser under 2 km on the nose.
- It sticks to that one ship until it's destroyed, reaches **shelter** (docked, landed, or within
  `SHELTER` = 6 km of a station or gate: traffic control's defences), jumps to hyperdrive, leaves
  the system, gets 100 km away, or 10 minutes pass. Then it goes safe and resumes its route.
- Keeping clear (`avoid` and the collision warning):
  - Every body has a margin 3 km above its highest ground; stations and gates have their size
    plus 1.5 km.
  - Closing speed is kept to what the ship can stop in the room left, counting the time to turn
    the engine round (3.2 s) and the body's gravity. Inside a margin it's pushed out and held up.
  - On top of that, the collision warning looks 25 s ahead, 4 times a second. If what the ship is
    doing hits something, it breaks off away from it at full thrust. This is the same module the
    player's ship has.
  - It stays in force while standing down after a hunt.
- Pirates use the same devices, commands, fire control and physics as everyone. Their
  transponder still says "Settler N", but anyone's radar shows `WEAPONS HOT` for an armed ship.

## What it took (crash rate at 1×, 100 settlers × 2 game hours, 13 pirates)

| Version | Pirate crashes |
|---|---|
| First version | 66 (at 20×) |
| + keep-out margins | 39 |
| + brake budget with turn-round and gravity | 28 |
| + collision warning while hunting | 14 |
| + collision warning while standing down | 10 |
| + shelter (no hunting landed ships or at gates/stations) | **4**, with 692 hunts and 125 kills |

Traders never crash. At 20× warp hunts are sloppier: crafts get one command per 0.33 s frame, so
there are more crashes and fewer kills. The collision checks also cost more there (1,000
settlers: 11.7 ms/frame at 20×; at 1× it's 0.32 ms for 100). The game runs at 1×.

## Kill feed

- sim `combat`: each kill by weapons fire is a `Kill { time, system, killer, victim, names,
  weapon }`, from the victim's last `Hit` and its destruction (`Universe::kills`, the last 50).
  Pirate kills are counted from who fired the last hit.
- HUD: below the perf corner, `SETTLER 12 DESTROYED SETTLER 40 - GUNFIRE`. It shows kills in the
  system in view and any involving you (in red), fading over 15 s.
- Dev scenario `pirates`: a pirate goes after a trader near you, and the feed shows the kill.

Tests: `a_pirate_hunts_down_a_trader_and_goes_back_to_its_route` (a kill in 39 s, then stand
down and resume the route). The long traffic run reports pirates, hunts, kills and crashes by
role. `debug_pirate_chase_through_gate` (ignored) traces a chase at a gate.
