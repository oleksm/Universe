# 2026-09-30 — Trading: goods, local markets, the hold, legality

User: "next big challenge is probably trading. Single currency, but market visibility needs to be
local, within the star system. A market action button. Generate 1,000 items. A single station
market doesn't have to host all: it has some subset of what it produces and what it wants. Traders
have to move to another system to find out what its market is. Our ship now has cargo. Each
station decides legality of items."

## World

- **`goods`**: a catalog of 1,000 items generated from the galaxy seed, 50 in each of 20
  categories: food, water, ores, metals, minerals, chemicals, fuel, textiles, machinery,
  electronics, computers, medicine, biologics, luxuries, art, weapons, narcotics, artifacts,
  robots and tools. Each has a distinct name from its category's word lists (adjective + noun), a
  base price (log-uniform in the category's range, from about 1 cr for water to 9,000 cr for
  alien artifacts) and a mass per unit (from 5 kg for narcotics to a tonne for ores and water).
  Same seed, same goods.
- **`market`**: every station and spaceport has a market, generated from the seed, the system and
  the facility, and leaning on what the place is:
  - stations make machinery, electronics, computers, robots, tools, medicine, fuel and chemicals,
    and want food, water, ores, metals, luxuries, textiles, biologics and art;
  - spaceports on Terran worlds (farms) make food, water, textiles, biologics, luxuries and art,
    and want machines, electronics, computers, medicine, tools, robots and fuel;
  - spaceports on dry or cratered worlds (mines) make ores, metals, minerals, chemicals, fuel and
    artifacts, and want food, water, machines, tools, medicine, robots and luxuries.

  Each makes about 27–42 items and wants about as many, including a few off-list ones.
  - **Bans (legality)**: each market bans categories, vice first: narcotics 70%, weapons 40%,
    artifacts 25%, biologics 10%, robots 10%. It doesn't trade them, and they show as ILLEGAL in
    your hold there.
  - **Prices**:
    - What it makes, it sells from stock at 0.55–0.9 × base, rising up to 1.6× as the stock runs
      down, and buys back at 70% of its selling price.
    - What it wants, it buys at 1.2–1.9 × base, falling to 0.4× as its demand is met.
    - Stock and demand recover toward their usual levels over about 6 game hours.
    - A market's state is kept only once it's been traded with. Heavier, cheaper goods come in
      bigger lots.
  - Trading is only possible while docked or landed at that market (`market::docked_at`).
- **The hold** (`Ship::hold`): units per item, and `Ship::cargo`, its mass, counted in the ship's
  mass. So a full hold makes the ship slower to accelerate, and the physics feels it. Capacity is
  `HOLD_CAPACITY` = 20 t.
- `World::{market, quotes, trade}`. `Facility` is now `Hash`.

## Sim and game

- **Credits**: one currency on the pilot (`Universe::credits`), starting at 1,000 and saved.
- `Universe::{markets, docked_market, market_quotes, trade}`.
  - `markets` lists only the system you're in: that's local visibility. To know another system's
    prices, go there.
- **Market screen (G)**, from the pilot's seat, with its own lamp on the action grid (lit when
  docked at a market):
  - The header shows the market's name and n/m in the system, whether trading is open ("DOCKED
    HERE") or view only, your credits, the hold's mass, and what's banned here.
  - The table shows goods, kind, whether it SELLS or WANTS, the price to buy at and to sell at,
    stock or demand left, kg per unit, and how many you hold. Hold items this market doesn't trade
    are listed dim, or red if banned.
  - Keys: ←/→ market, ↑/↓ and PgUp/PgDn item, + or ENTER buy, - or DEL sell (SHIFT: 10 at a
    time), G or ESC close.
- The flight HUD's mass line shows cargo tonnes and credits.
- Dev scenario `market`: docked at the home station with the market open, after buying 10 of
  something.

Test: `markets_are_local_varied_and_prices_move_with_trade` (markets differ, reproducible,

mass; selling back pays less; stock recovers in a day; banned goods are refused), and

## Follow-up: traders trade, and the trade feed

User: "are our traders actually trading? Let's create a trade event: who buys/sells what, how many,
current cargo load, current credits", then "make another 10% slice of settlers become traders".

- **Roles**: from each settler's seed, 1 in 10 is a pirate, another 1 in 10 a trader
  (`Craft::trader`), and the rest just travel.
- **Traders** (sim `commerce`) start with 3,000 credits and keep track of what they paid per unit.
  At each route stop (docked or landed) they:
  1. **Sell** what the market takes, when it wants it or when the price beats what they paid by
     5%. With the hold over 80% full they take the buy-back even at a loss.
  2. **Buy** up to three bargains, goods selling under 85% of their worth, in categories this
     market doesn't want itself (goods flow from where they're made to where they're wanted),
     within 30% of their credits each, half the stock, and the room left in the hold.

  Like the pilot, a trader sees only the market it's at.
- **Markets want kinds of goods, not just their list**: `Market::wants` (the categories it leans
  toward). Unlisted goods of those kinds are bought at `GENERAL_PREMIUM` = 1.1 × worth, with steady
  demand. Listed ones keep their better prices. (`Market::offer_for` / `quote_for`,
  `World::quote_for`.) Before this, a trader's goods almost never appeared on the next market's
  short list: the holds filled up and the credits drained (average 3,000 → 969 in 3 h). Now the
  average is about 2,000 in credits plus cargo, the best 5,580 after 3 game hours: an uneven
  market, as it should be. The market screen shows these general prices for what's in your hold.
- **Trade records** (`TradeRecord`: time, system, market, trader, bought or sold, item, units,
  amount, cargo and credits after) are kept in `Universe::trade_log`, the last 100. Your own trades
  are logged too, as "YOU". `TrafficStats::{trades, turnover}`.
- **HUD trade feed**, right side below the kills:
  `SETTLER 357 BOUGHT 16 POLISHED GARNETS FOR 790 CR - CARGO 19.9 T, 1933 CR`. It shows the
  system in view and your own trades, fading over 12 s. The traffic line counts trades and kills.
- Dev scenario `trades`; ignored test `settlers_trade` (trades, turnover, and the spread of trader
  wealth after 3 game hours).

## Follow-up: traders plan trips by profit, and a saner economy

User: "traders should choose their next route based on the profit margins, so they can analyse the
market and then go. If they can't find a good market at all, they may try to move to a different
star system."

- **Trip planning** (`commerce::plan_trip`): at each stop, after selling, a trader looks over every
  other market in the system (local knowledge, as for the pilot). For each one it counts:
  - what its cargo would fetch there, over what it paid;
  - what it could buy here to sell there: the best margins per kilo first, up to 3 lines, within
    its credits, the hold, half the stock here, and the demand there.

  It buys for the best trip and goes there (the destination is appended to its route, flown after
  the usual dwell).
- If no trip promises `MIN_PROFIT` = 300 cr, it **moves on**: through a gate to a neighbouring
  system (seeded choice), to one of its markets, and plans again there. Its first route is just
  the start; after the first stop it goes where the money is.
- **Decisions are logged too**: `Deal::{Bought, Sold, Heading { to, expect }, MovingOn { to }}`. The
  feed shows `SETTLER 357 HEADS FOR PORT ATES (LERICEEN C) - EXPECTS +1807 CR` and
  `… FINDS NOTHING HERE - MOVES ON TO USSODIVE`.
- **Economy fix**: planning found a runaway. One trader went from 3,000 to 2.17 million credits in
  3 game hours, selling 909 therapies at once. Lots were sized by mass, so light valuable goods
  came in thousands, and spreads were up to 3× per trip. Now:
  - a market's usual stock or demand per line is about `LOT_VALUE` = 8,000 cr worth (3–400
    units);
  - makers sell at 0.75–0.95 × worth, and wanters pay 1.05–1.35 ×;
  - general demand for wanted kinds is half a lot at 1.05 × worth.

  With this, 9 traders × 3 game hours average 6,500 cr (from 3,000), max about 17,900, with trips
  of about 1,000–2,500 cr expected profit. No one needed to move on within 3 hours, since there are
  about 17 markets per system; that happens as markets get drained.
