# 2026-09-30 — Re-architecture R3: the pilot interface

User: "Yes" (carry on with R3).

Avionics are pilots. They now see the world only through their bus and act only through it, and
a test holds them to it.

## The `Bus`, as the pilot interface

- **Sensors:** the ship's own instruments, its system, the time, the charts and where the
  bodies are, the turrets it can see.
- **The feed (`feed`):** the physical events since it was last read. Before, they came back as
  the return value of a command, as if the pilot were inside the world.
- **Actuation (`actuate`):** device settings, with nothing returned.
- **Traffic control:** `request_clearance(target)` (granted for what, or refused with the
  reason), `clearance_holds(target)`, `request_pad`, `request_corridor`.
  - Clearance is now decided on the world's side by traffic control's rules
    (`world::traffic::request`, `lapsed`, `nearest_station`). Before, the pilot ran those rules
    itself.
  - `PadGrant` (a traffic control reply) is in `universe-protocol`.

## Implementations (sim)

- `Link` (the player, and requests made outside the tick) and `FrameLink` (crafts stepping side
  by side) each keep the devices' events for the feed, and answer clearance with traffic
  control's rules.
- Under the command delay, actuation goes into the craft's inbox. The feed then reports what the
  devices do when the commands arrive.

## Enforced

`avionics/tests/boundary.rs` reads the avionics sources (test modules aside) and fails on world
internals:
- the `World` itself;
- traffic control and its rules (`pads`, `traffic::request`, `lapsed`, `nearest_station`);
- damage, markets, collisions, turret placement.

What's allowed: the shared charts and their geometry, published hardware specs, the contracts,
the physics kernel (to predict), and published contact rules. I checked the test by planting a
`World` reference: it names the file and line.
