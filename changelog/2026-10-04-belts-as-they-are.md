# Belts as they are (the engine's part)

- **Every system has its belts as the Sun's are:**
  - the main belt between the first gas giant's resonances, with the Kirkwood gaps left empty;
  - both trojan swarms of every giant (a cloud within 5% of its orbit, 26° either side of its
    point, as many as the giant's mass to Jupiter's);
  - the outer belt past the outermost giant.

  Counts are by the registry's `seeding.asteroids` and the ground each belt covers. Treistun's
  main belt has 141,764 rocks over 1 km (the registry's figure: about 142,000) and 400 million
  over 15 m.
- **Every rock goes round the star on its own orbit, made from the seed only where something
  looks.** Rocks lie on rings of one exact orbit size, so a ring turns as one and a patch of it
  stays the same rocks for ever. Each size class (a decade of size) has rings spaced so a patch
  holds some 64 rocks. "The rocks near here, now" is found in milliseconds anywhere: about five
  rocks within 200,000 km of a point in Treistun's main belt, the nearest some 90,000 km off.
  Rocks are hours apart, as they are.
- **Simplified:** a small rock's orbit is nearly round (only the biggest reach the eccentricities
  of 0.1 to 0.2 real belt rocks have). A trojan swarm's rings off its giant's exact orbit drift
  from its point, a twentieth of a turn in some ten of the giant's years.
- **Not in play yet:** the fields of fragments round a remnant are still what ships mine. Belt
  rocks join play next. `registry-figures` writes each charted system's belt counts.
