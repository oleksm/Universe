# Describing a complete civilization in the registry: the plan

*Registry session, 2026-10-04. The vision is `docs/vision-civilization.md`. This says, for each
part of it, what has to exist as records before the game can make it real: the things, the places
that make them, who owns them, and the rules. The registry describes what things are and the most
they can do; what people then do with them is the game's.*

## How a concept becomes matter

Every idea in the vision has to land as one of six kinds of record, or it stays a wish.

| Kind of record | Says | Today |
|---|---|---|
| **An item** (good, stock, part, equipment) | what a thing is: its mass, size, how it keeps, what it is traded as | 106 goods, 22 stock items, 185 parts, 57 pieces of equipment, 20 market categories |
| **A module with recipes** | what makes it, from what, how fast, on what power | 50 modules (metal, the yard, food) |
| **A structure or a hull** | the big things modules and people are housed in or carried by | 6 hulls, 4 structures, 3 gate rings |
| **An organisation** | who owns, makes, runs, governs | 23 companies, a standards body, an administration |
| **A place** (settlement, zone, parcel, facility) | where it stands and on whose land | 14 settlements, a rig, 20 zones, 24 facilities |
| **A rule** (law, standard, code) | what holds for everyone: nature's, the trade's, the administration's | 31 laws of Dogma, 18 standards, a zoning code |

Two things the vision needs have no kind yet, and are proposed below: **what a person needs** and
**what a person can do**.

## Four rules that hold throughout

1. **Nothing is made of nothing.** Every recipe balances by mass. A thing given off is a thing:
   it has a record, and something should take it.
2. **Everything is somewhere.** A thing made lies in a store until it is used, sold or hauled.
3. **Things wear out.** A complete world is one where what is built has a life and must be made
   again. Without this the matter loop is a line, not a loop.
4. **Sourced, worked out, or a guess that says so.** As everywhere in the registry.

## The two missing kinds

### What a person needs (`need`)

The ladder, as records. Each need: what it is, which rung it is on, what meets it (items, or a
place to be), how much a person a day, and what happens when it is not met.

| Rung | Needs (a first list) |
|---|---|
| Alive | air (0.84 kg of oxygen a day), water (drinking, washing), food (1.5 kg), warmth, shelter (floor area), medicine |
| Together | a home of one's own, clothes, safety, power and light, news |
| Better | schooling, tools of a trade, a way to travel |
| To matter | art, sport and play, drink and company, worship, travel for its own sake |

This is the source for the game's "household basket", and the measure a settlement is held to:
**is every need of its people met by something made here or brought here?**

### What a person can do (`profession`)

The trades: farmer, miner, smelter, machinist, welder, pilot, doctor, teacher, clerk, judge,
constable, banker, artist. Each: what it takes to learn (time, where), and what it is needed for.
A module then says how many of which trade it takes to run. That one link is what turns a
population into a workforce, a workforce into wages, and schooling into something worth having.

## The seven parts, in the order to describe them

Each is described the same way: its items, its modules and recipes, its structures, its
organisations, its rules. The order follows the ladder, so that at each step a settlement could
stand on what has been described so far.

### 1. Body: staying alive

| Needs | Have | To describe |
|---|---|---|
| Food | 57 foods, 18 farm and food modules | done but for placing them |
| Water | water as a good, won from asteroid ice | wells, ice melting, water works, a water main; drinking water against raw; waste water and its treatment |
| Air | nothing | for a sealed settlement: air plant (oxygen from water or from plants, carbon dioxide scrubbed), the air a hull or a hall leaks; on a world with air, none needed |
| Power | fusion power station, deuterium | where deuterium comes from (a plant that takes it from water: 1 t in 31,000 t); smaller power than 400 MW; the heat it sheds |
| Homes | nothing | dwelling blocks (floor area a person), residential zones, what a home draws (water, power, heat) |
| Clothes | a market category | fibre (cotton, flax, wool: crops and animals already half there), spinning, weaving, a clothing works |
| Health | a market category | clinic, hospital, a pharma works; medicines as items |
| Waste | manure, straw, offal, slag, scrap as items | what takes each: compost, rendering, a recycler; a sewage works. **Report: every thing given off has a taker.** |

About 40 goods, 25 modules, 3 structures (dwelling block, clinic, hospital).

### 2. People and work

| Needs | Have | To describe |
|---|---|---|
| Who lives where | the game's four sizes of place | a settlement's population, in the record (the integrator has asked for it) |
| Trades | nothing | the `profession` records: 30 to 40 |
| Who runs what | nothing | each module's crew: how many of which trade. **Report: a settlement's jobs against its people.** |

This is the hinge. Until a module needs people, a population is only mouths.

### 3. Making: the matter loop

About 60 goods and stock items, 40 modules. The largest part. **Report: for each made thing, a

## How we will know a part is done

A report for each loop, in the registry's page, each with gaps to close:

| Report | Asks |
|---|---|
| Needs | Is each need of a person met by an item or a place that is described? |
| Chains | Does each made thing trace back to rock, air or water? (Exists for the MC-07 and the gate ring.) |
| Takers | Is each thing given off taken by something? |
| Wear | Does each made thing say how long it lasts and what replaces it? |
| Work | Does each module say who runs it? Does each trade have somewhere it is learned? |
| A settlement | Its people's needs against what is made there; its jobs against its people; its stores against a week of use. |

## What waits on the engine

The engine's types refuse what they do not know, so these are asked for, not added:

- a module's crew; a settlement's population and structure;
- zone uses beyond the five (agricultural, and what parts 5 to 7 need);
- facility kinds beyond the five (a farm, a water works, a school, a clinic...): better one field
  saying what a facility is for than a list that grows with every part;
- how a module says it stands under an open sky.

New kinds (`need`, `profession`) the engine ignores until it reads them: they can go in now.

## Order of work, and why

1. **`need` and `profession`** first: small, new kinds, and everything after is measured against them.
2. **Body**, less food: water, air, power's fuel, homes, clothes, health, waste.
3. **People and work:** crews for the 50 modules there are.
4. **Making:** building materials, then chemicals, then the parked metals, then machines and electronics; wear and recycling with each.
5. **Moving:** the hulls.
6. **Order, money, mind and spirit:** mostly places and organisations, quick once the above stands.

Each part: describe, look up the figures, mark the guesses, run its report.

## Decided (the user, 2026-10-04)

- Trades are broad: a farmer, not a ploughman.
- Everything made has a life.
- Worship: to come back to. Hulls: skipped for now.

## Done

**1. Needs and professions (2026-10-04).** A new root, `standards/People/`: 20 needs on the four
rungs (each with what a person uses and gives off a day, the floor and power it takes, whose work
meets it, how long one can go without) and 36 trades (each with how it is learned and how long
that takes). Two reports: **Needs** (6 of 20 are met by something described: air, water, food,
warmth, power; the other 14 are the work ahead) and **Work**. Sourced since (`standards/sources/research_people_needs.json`): 12 of the 20 needs
rest on a published figure (NASA's for a crew, the WHO's, the World Bank's); art, play, news, tools,
travel and the weight of medicines are still guesses. A person balances: 4.9 kg a day in (oxygen,
water, food), 4.9 out (carbon dioxide, waste water).

**2. Body (2026-10-04).** 12 of 20 needs are now met by something described (11 of the 13 on the
first two rungs; medicines and somewhere for a constable and a judge are what is left there).

| What | Records |
|---|---|
| Water | Ice melter (asteroid ice to water, 776 kJ a kg), water works (waste water back to drinking water, 2.1 kWh a m3) |
| Air | Air plant (carbon dioxide and hydrogen to water and methane); with the hydrogen plant it closes breath without plants. Halls close it with them |
| Fuel | Deuterium plant: 4.97 kg of water and 13.9 MWh for each kg of deuterium. One feeds twenty power stations |
| Homes | A new kind, `building`: bunkhouse (240 people), dwelling block (2,000), each with floor, weight, life and the needs it meets |
| Health | Clinic and hospital, with their staff by trade. Nothing makes medicines yet: that waits on chemicals |
| Clothes | Seed cotton and flax in the field, wool in the barn; textile mill (three cloths), clothing works (clothes, woollens, linens) |
| Waste | Compost works (compost; growing medium for the halls, new and cleaned), rendering works (tallow, meat and bone meal) |
| Food | The hydroponic hall: `docs/registry-food.md` |

A new report, **Takers**: of 30 things given off, 10 are taken by something. The 20 that are not
(scrap of three metals, slag, dross, red mud, methane, compost, sewage sludge, cottonseed, whey,
pomace and others) are the recycling work of part 3.

**Left open in Body, and why:**

- **Wells, and mines on the ground.** A recipe needs an input, and the ground's is what the world
  is made of, which the user is bringing later. Until then water comes from asteroid ice and from
  cleaning what has been used.
- **Medicines.** No figure for what a person uses was found, and what they are made from is
  chemicals, part 3.
- **Power smaller than 400 MW.** Not described.
- **How long clothes last** (3.3 years, found) has nowhere to go: a good has no life yet. With
  wear, part 3.
- **Homes in vacuum.** A building does not say whether it holds air.
- **A building's draw on a hot or cold world:** as the hall's, asked of the engine.

**3. Making, first step: building materials and scrap (2026-10-04).**

| What | Records |
|---|---|
| Rock | Limestone, clay and silica sand: found only on worlds that have had liquid water (as bauxite). Aggregate is crushed from asteroid rock or from furnace slag |
| Works | Crushing plant, lime kiln (lime had no maker), cement works, concrete plant, glassworks, brickworks |
| Buildings | Each now says what it is built of: 1,040 kg of concrete and 58 kg of steel for each m2 of floor |
| Scrap | Steel scrap goes back into the arc furnace, aluminium scrap into the casthouse (5% of the energy of new metal) |

Takers: 14 of 30 now have one. **What this shows:** cement, glass and brick need rock that only
a wet world has. A station or an airless port must ship them in, or build of something else
(steel, or rock melted or sintered, which is not described). Soda ash for glass has no maker yet:
chemicals, next.
