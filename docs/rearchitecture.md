# Universe — Re-architecture plan: core, services, clients

Status: **adopted** (2026-09-30); decisions in §10 accepted as proposed. R0–R4 done. `docs/architecture.md`
describes the code as it is; this describes where it goes and in what order. Once a phase
lands, its part moves into `architecture.md`.

The aim is to settle scale and accountability once, by putting every piece of state and every
decision with exactly one owner, behind a contract that holds at 10× today's load and with
untrusted participants (multiplayer).

---

## 1. The three roles

| | **Core** (the body and the physics) | **Services** (the institutions) | **Clients** (the minds) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is | The physical world: bodies, mass, motion, forces, contacts, the hardware of ships | The world's social machinery: traffic control, markets, the ledger, aggression and law, defence, dispatch, records | Pilots: the player's client, and pooled NPC pilots; turret gunners too |
| Knows | Objects, positions, velocities, orientations, masses, hardware state and limits | Their own domain's state, and what the core and messages tell them | Only what their sensors return and what messages they receive |
| Decides | Nothing. It integrates inputs under fixed rules | Their own domain: grants, prices, transfers, legal status | Everything a pilot decides: where to go, what program flies, fight or flight, trade or not |
| Changes | Physical state only | Their own authoritative records, and core state through audited operations | Nothing authoritative. They only request |
| Trust | Trusted | Trusted, and accountable (every change has a recorded cause) | **Untrusted**, even in-process NPC pools: same contract as a remote player |
| Runs | Its own thread(s), at a fixed tick | Actors on worker threads, each at its own pace | Own threads (player: render + world link; NPCs: pools) |

The core never waits for a service or a client. A late participant only delays its own effect
on the world.

## 2. Contracts

### 2.1 Core ← clients: actuation

Per body a client controls, stamped with the tick it's meant for:

- **Main engine** throttle, **thrusters** vector, **lift**, **turn** rates (or a commanded attitude
  rate).
- **Weapons**: master arm, triggers, gun aim (gimbal).
- **Hyperdrive**: engage or disengage, and its steering while engaged.
- Later: power routing, landing gear, cargo release.

Rules:

- **The core clamps every request to the hardware.** That covers thrust and fuel, turn rate,
  gimbal range, heat, arming time, the hyperdrive jam, and damaged hardware. Asking for more
  than the hardware can give gets what the hardware gives. This is the "software can't cheat"
  principle, enforced in one place.
- **Actuator settings hold** until changed. A silent client's ship keeps doing what it was told
  (see §6 for a dead-man rule).
- **A client may command only the bodies it is bound to**: a pilot binding is granted by a
  service (§2.4).

### 2.2 Core ← services: operations

Audited, each with a cause (§5):

- **Spawn and despawn** bodies: a delivered ship, a jettisoned crate, a wreck.
- **Lock and release**: lock a body to another body (docked, landed), or let it go (launch,
  lift-off).
- **Relocate**: gate transit, hand-over between star frames.
- **Hardware changes**: fit or remove a module, refuel, rearm, repair, set cargo mass.
- **Contact rules** (§2.5): registered ahead of time, executed by the core.

### 2.3 Core → everyone: what happened

Each tick, the core publishes:

- **The tick snapshot**: immutable and shared (zero-copy in-process). It holds every body's
  state and a spatial index over them.
- **The event log**, tick-stamped and numbered:
  - contacts, and contact rules fired (locked, bounced);
  - hits, with the energy deposited, the projectile and its source body;
  - transits, hardware failures (overheat, destroyed), spawns and despawns.
- **Sensor returns**, per subscribed body: generated by the core from that body's sensor
  hardware (radar range and resolution, line of sight). They're what a pilot's radar shows,
  never more.

Readers don't get the world: they get queries over the snapshot, limited by interest (§4.3).

### 2.4 Services ↔ everyone: messages

A message system separate from the core (it can later move out of process):

- **Every message carries** an id, sender, recipient(s), the tick it was sent at, and the id of
  what caused it (a core event, or another message).
- **Delivery:** at least once, in order per sender. **Handlers must be idempotent**: a repeat
  of a seen id is ignored.
- **Requests and replies:** clearance requests and grants, trade orders and fills, hails and
  distress calls, bindings (this pilot may fly that ship), posse calls between NPCs.
- **Services subscribe** to core events: the law service to hits, ATC to locks at its station,
  the ledger to cargo transfers.

### 2.5 Contact rules as data

Decisions that must happen *at* the moment of contact can't wait for a service. Services
register them in advance and the core applies them blindly. For example:

- A station's docking port: lock a body arriving through this cut-out at under 2 m/s, aligned
  within 5°, if it holds a valid grant token issued by ATC. Otherwise bounce, or (too fast)
  deposit the energy as damage.
- A spaceport pad: lock a body touching down at under 4 m/s and tilted under 10°.
- A gate's ring: relocate a body passing the opening under 300 m/s to its linked gate.
  Otherwise destroy it.

Rules are parameters on structures, versioned, and every firing is logged. This replaces
today's game logic inside contact handling (`station::contact`, `spaceport::touch_down`,
`gate::enter`).

## 4. Scale

Targets this design must meet without another rework:

- **Now:** 10,000 ships, 60 Hz, one machine.
- **Headroom:** 100,000 ships, many star systems, and tens of players per system.

### 4.1 The core

- **Shard by star system.** Systems are nearly independent; only transits and hand-overs cross
  between them, as relocations queued between ticks.
- **Within a system, partition space** (cells sized to a tick's motion plus reach). Integration
  runs in parallel per body. The collision search runs per cell and its neighbours, only where
  something moves (already done).
- **The tick is a pipeline:**
  1. apply actuation due this tick;
  2. integrate (parallel);
  3. contacts and contact rules;
  4. projectiles;
  5. publish the snapshot and events.

  No participant runs inside it.
- **Rails bodies** (planets, stations) are a pure function of time: computed once per tick per
  system and shared.

### 4.2 Clients (NPC pilots)

- **Pools**: a few worker threads (about one per core, configurable), ships shared out across
  them by system and proximity for cache locality.
- **Think-rate levels of detail:** each pilot says when it next needs to think.
  - A docking run thinks every tick.
  - Cruising thinks every 0.5–1 s.
  - Docked and idle thinks on a timer or a message.

  Cost scales with *activity*, not with ship count.
- **Pools read the newest snapshot** and post actuation stamped for tick N+k. They never block
  the core.

### 4.3 Interest management (the trap to avoid)

Nobody receives the whole world:

- **Sensor returns** are per body, within its radar's range, from the spatial index.
- **Services subscribe** to their region (ATC: its zone) or to event kinds (law: hits).
- **The player's client** gets its sensor returns, plus a display feed of what its camera can
  plausibly see.
- **In-process:** queries over the shared immutable snapshot, with no copies. **Over a
  network:** deltas per subscription.

### 4.4 Services

- **One actor per institution instance** (a station's ATC, a market), each owning its state,
  running on a worker pool, communicating only by messages.
- **No service holds a lock across another service.** Cross-service work is a message
  exchange, such as a trade: the market reserves, the ledger transfers, the market confirms.

## 5. Accountability

Every authoritative change can answer *who, when, why*:

- **Causes are recorded.** Each core operation and each service state change carries the tick
  and the id of the message or event that caused it. A wreck traces back to the hit event,
  which traces to the projectile, the firing body, and the actuation that pulled the trigger
  (with its client).
- **The ledger is double-entry.** Every transfer of credits or goods is a balanced entry with
  its cause, so money and cargo can't appear or vanish.
- **Law is evidence-based.** Aggression is assigned only from core hit events. The record keeps
  the event id, the target's status at the time, and the rule applied. Clearing it is also a
  logged decision.
- **Inputs are logged.** The core's input log (actuation and operations, by tick) plus the seed
  reproduces the physical world exactly: replays, debugging, dispute resolution, and tests.
- **Clients can't write authority.** The only paths to change the world are actuation (clamped
  by hardware) and requests (decided by services). A malicious client can, at worst, fly its
  own ship badly or spam requests, which are rate-limited per binding.
- **Bindings are explicit.** Which client flies which ship is a record held by a service,
  granted and revoked with a cause. Turret gunners are bound to turrets in the same way.

## 6. Time, ordering, failure

- **Fixed tick** (60 Hz now; configurable). World time advances only in the core.
- **Input deadline k** (proposal: k = 2 ticks):
  - A client reading snapshot N stamps actuation for N+k.
  - Inputs arriving on time are applied exactly at their tick, so the world is deterministic
    for on-time participants.
  - Late inputs are applied at the next tick, flagged in the input log.
  - Inputs older than a horizon are dropped.
- **Messages** are ordered per sender; across senders, order is decided by the service
  receiving them, deterministically: by tick, then sender id, then sequence number.
- **A silent client:** the ship holds its controls. **Dead-man rule (proposal):** after T
  seconds with no input from its binding, the hardware cuts the main engine and thrusters and
  makes weapons safe. It's a core rule, visible to everyone.
- **A service fails or restarts:** it rebuilds its state from its own persisted records, plus
  the event log since then. Handlers are idempotent, so replaying is safe.
- **Back-pressure:** queues are bounded. An overloaded pool degrades its own pilots' think rate
  and never the core tick.

---

## 7. Testing

- **Core:** scripted actuation per tick, with assertions on physical outcomes. Determinism test:
  the same input log gives the same state hash.
- **Lockstep harness:** pools and services run synchronously, with the tick waiting for all of
  them. Today's fast interaction tests run on it unchanged in spirit: two ships at a gate, pads
  full, posse, turrets.
- **Fault injection:** late inputs, dropped messages, a restarted service, a silent client. The
  world stays consistent and the cause records stay complete.
- **Load:** bounded, short runs at 10k and 100k bodies, measuring tick time, never long
  simulations (standing rule).
- **Contract tests per service:** message in, records and messages out, idempotence.

## 8. Target crate layout

```
physics      kernel: integration, contacts, rays, atmosphere (unchanged role)
core         bodies, hardware and its limits, contact rules, operations, sensors, the tick,
             snapshot, event log, input log, spatial index
protocol     the contracts: actuation, operations, events, sensor returns, messages, ids
bus          message system: delivery, ordering, idempotence, subscriptions
services/    atc, market, ledger, law, defence (turret gunners' pool), records, dispatch
pilot        avionics programs, the stick arbiter, prediction (uses physics), NPC minds
             (trader, settler, pirate, defender), the NPC pool runner
charts       galaxy, systems, gates, goods (shared, read-only)
client       the player's: input → intents, the pilot stack for the player's ship, view, HUD
engine       rendering, windowing, audio (unchanged role)
host         wires it together in one process (single player); later: server and client
             binaries
```

Dependencies point down only:
- `pilot` and `services` depend on `protocol` (and `physics` for prediction), **never on
  `core` internals**;
- `core` depends on `physics` and `protocol`;
- `client` depends on `pilot`, `protocol` and `engine`.

## 9. Migration (each phase ships, keeps the game playable, and has an exit test)

| # | Phase | What moves | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 ✓ | **Contracts** | `protocol` crate: actuation, operations, events, sensor returns, messages, ids, causes. No behaviour change | Types reviewed; the current code compiles against them where it already fits |
| R1 ✓ | **Autopilot out of the integrator** | Remove `FlightComputer::substep` and the hyperdrive callback. Actuation changes only at tick boundaries. Retune docking, landing, gate and hyperdrive guidance for 60 Hz plus k-tick delay | All interaction tests pass with commands held per tick and an artificial 2-tick delay |
| R2 ✓ | **Contact rules as data** | Docking port, pads and gate become registered rules. The core keeps no station or spaceport logic | Docking, landing and gate tests pass. Rule firings appear in the event log |
| R3 ✓ | **Pilot interface** | Avionics behind sensor-packet-in, actuation-and-requests-out, run synchronously (lockstep). Pilots lose direct world access | No avionics module imports world internals. Tests green in lockstep |
| R4 ✓ | **Services out of the world** | ATC, market, ledger (credits and hold contents), law (aggression from hit events), records; the message bus with causes. Weapons code stops assigning aggression | Every authoritative change has a cause record. Ledger balances. Tests green |
| R5 | **Turrets as clients** | The defence service's gunner pool aims and fires through actuation | Turret tests green, turrets lag only themselves |
| R6 | **Asynchronous NPC pools** | Pools on worker threads, the k-tick deadline, think-rate levels of detail, late-input handling, dead-man rule | Tick time independent of pool load. Determinism holds with on-time pools. Load test at 10k |
| R7 | **The player as a client** | The player's computers (radar picture, fire control, plan, collision warning, follow) move to the client, with prediction from the shared kernel | The same contract for the player as for NPCs. Docking by autopilot works at 2-tick delay |
| R8 | **Interest management and sharding** | Spatial index queries for sensors and subscriptions; per-system core shards; partition within a system | Tick time at 100k bodies within budget (short load test). No full-world copies on any path |
| R9 | **Persistence and audit** | World save = core state + input-log checkpoint; service records; per-pilot saves; replay tool | Replay of a recorded session reproduces the state hash |

Order rationale:
- R1 and R2 first: they remove the deepest entanglements (pilots inside physics, game rules
  inside contacts), and every later phase depends on that.
- R3 and R4 then split ownership while everything is still synchronous, so tests stay
  meaningful.
- Asynchrony (R6 and R7) comes only after the contracts are proven.

## 10. Decisions (accepted as proposed, 2026-09-30)

1. **Input deadline k** (proposal 2 ticks = 33 ms at 60 Hz), and the late-input horizon.
2. **Dead-man rule:** on or off, and T (proposal: 30 s).
3. **Hardware vs software modules:** proposal: radar, gimbal, thrusters, hyperdrive and armour
   are hardware (core); autopilots, planner, fire-control software and nav aids are software
   (client). Purchases of either go through the ledger. Fitting hardware is a core operation.
4. **Tick rate:** stay at 60 Hz, or decouple: physics at 60, sensor publishing at 20–30 for
   NPCs?
5. **Walking crew:** a full core body (proposed), or client-side and validated.
6. **Message bus scope now:** in-process only, with the network as a later transport behind
   the same interface (proposed).

Also decided while starting R1: **a tick is a fixed slice of game time** (1/60 s at the
world's time scale). Single-player warp (a dev tool) runs more ticks per frame, up to a
budget; beyond it, ticks stretch and the sim reports it can't keep up. Control rates
therefore stay in game time whatever the warp.
